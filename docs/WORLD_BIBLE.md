# World Bible — The Vell and the Meridian Canal

Every element here exists to create card conditions, conflicts or consequences. Lore with no gameplay hook has been cut.

## 1. History (public version)
- **Year 0 of the Canal (Y0)**: The Aldmere Parliament of Guilds and the Sorrel Assembly grant a joint charter to the **Meridian Canal Company** to cut a water road across the Spine. Chief engineer: **Ysolde Marrow**.
- **Y0–Y11**: Construction. Eighteen locks (nine each side) climb to the **Summit Pound**. The **Ninefold Reservoirs** are dug in the high valleys; the **Cald Tarn aqueduct** is built to feed the pound. Marrow dies in Y11, a month before the opening, of "a fever taken in the works".
- **Y12**: Opening. Grain flows west, coal and iron flow east. The village at the top Aldmere gate becomes the town of **Halfway**.
- **Y31**: The **Dry Year**. The pound falls to the sills; the canal closes for a season. The Company blames drought, raises tolls, and creates the office of **Summit Auditor**.
- **Y44**: The **Hullfolk Clearance**. The Company orders the boat-clans off the Summit Pound moorings. Riots at Halfway; a Keeper is killed; the order is rescinded but the clans are confined to the **Reed Reach** at the pound's west end.
- **Y58**: Aldmere and Sorrel nearly go to war over a seized coal fleet. The **Halfway Compact** allows each shore an inspector at the summit.
- **Y77**: Keeper **Ansel Dray** appointed.
- **Y80 (game start)**: Dray vanishes. The Company appoints a new Keeper. The **Jubilee** of the canal's eightieth year is planned for the summit.

## 2. History (the truth, known to the narrative system)
- The Ninefold Reservoirs never held enough. Marrow's survey in Y6 showed the pound would dry every third summer.
- Marrow secretly drove a tunnel, the **Marrow Cut**, from the upper **River Quell** through the Spine into the pound, diverting most of the river.
- The Quell had watered the valley of **Lowmere**, west of the Spine. Over ten years Lowmere dried, its farms failed and its people took to boats on the canal that had drunk their river. They became the **Hullfolk**. Most Hullfolk no longer know exactly why they left; their songs do.
- Marrow did not die of a fever. She drowned in the Cut during a collapse in Y11 while trying to build a regulating sluice. The Company recorded a fever and sealed the sluice house.
- The Dry Year (Y31) was the first partial collapse of the Cut. The Company cleared it secretly under cover of "reservoir dredging" and created the Auditor to watch Keepers, because a Keeper who reads water levels carefully will eventually notice the reservoirs do not explain the pound.
- The Cut is silting again. Water falls about one mark per watch. Within a few years the canal will fail.
- Ansel Dray found Marrow's sluice-house key, entered the Cut and did not return. His body is there. His daughter Mirren does not know this.
- The current Summit Auditor, **Odile Vosk**, knows the whole truth and believes keeping it is the only way to keep the canal, the town and the peace.

## 3. Geography (spatial structure that matters)
```
 SORREL (west, industrial)                                  ALDMERE (east, agricultural)
   Locks 1–9 west  ─── Summit Pound (Reed Reach ... Halfway) ─── Lock No. 9 HALFWAY ─── Locks 8–1 east
                             │                       │
                    Cald Tarn aqueduct         Sluice house (sealed)
                    Ninefold Reservoirs        ↓ the Marrow Cut (secret) ← River Quell
                                               ↓ Lowmere (dry valley, west)
```
Places used in cards:
- **The Chamber**: the lock itself; two vessels can fit if short; the Pile-Up ending happens here.
- **The Keeper's House**: where petitioners come. Has Dray's study.
- **The Toll Office** and **Toll Book**.
- **The Telegraph Office** (Fennick).
- **The Still Water Chapel** and its undercroft, which floods at high Water.
- **The Wheel & Windlass** inn and brewery (Ilse); the brewery well tastes of iron.
- **Tamm's Boatyard**.
- **The Reed Reach**: Hullfolk moorings at the far end of the pound.
- **The Sluice House**: a locked stone building on the pound's north bank; officially a disused reservoir valve.
- **The Boundary Stone**: where the town marches out a Keeper it has rejected.
- **Cald Tarn**: the high lake; the aqueduct is visibly dry in late summer.

## 4. Institutions
- **Meridian Canal Company**: Board in the capital of neither shore (the neutral port of Ardent). Locally: Superintendent (Pell), Summit Auditor (Vosk), Engineer (Quenn), Keepers, lock hands.
- **Keeper's Powers**: open/close gates, set order of passage, levy statutory tolls, inspect cargo, refuse passage, impound, log incidents, requisition town labor in emergencies, keep the Ledger.
- **Aldmere Customs Post** at Halfway (Crane) under the Compact.
- **Sorrel Freight Agency** (Vane) and **Sorrel Escort Company** (Ferrant), the latter a private militia.
- **Town Moot** of Halfway, chaired by the reeve (Ilse).
- **Hullfolk clan law**: "holding" (mooring right), "water debt", "the remembered".
- **The Concordance of the Still Water**: chapel order that blesses vessels and keeps the "spring".

## 5. Technology level
Steam tugs and horse-drawn barges; telegraph between locks and to both shores; oil and gas lamps; printing; rifles; cast iron paddle gear; no electricity in homes, no motor vehicles, no flight, no photography beyond slow plates (the Company files use ink portraits).

## 6. Everyday life at Halfway
The town lives by the watch bell. Lock hands turn the paddles; the Keeper decides. Boats queue in the pound and pay in coin or Company scrip. The inn sells Iron Spring ale. Winter closes the west locks for a month; summer lowers the pound. Children are told to stay away from the sluice house.

## 7. Belief systems
- **The Concordance** holds that still water is holy and that the summit springs are a gift to be tended. Rites: blessing of vessels, the Lamp Vigil at low water. It was founded with a donation from Marrow in Y10; the founding charter mentions "the water I have given".
- **Hullfolk remembrance**: names of the dead are spoken at the Reed Reach; the *Lowmere Song* names "the river that went into the hill".
- **Company rationalism**: the canal is engineering and accounts; Pell believes it sincerely.

## 8. Laws that create cards
- Statutory toll table (Coffers vs Traffic).
- Cargo inspection right (Company/Aldmere vs Traffic/Sorrel).
- Order of passage: Company mail first, then by arrival; Keeper may vary "for the good of the works".
- The Compact: each shore may inspect; arms require both inspectors' seals.
- Clearance Order Y44 (rescinded, but printed copies exist and Pell would like to revive it).
- Mooring fees on the pound; Hullfolk claim exemption by "holding".
- Keeper's emergency requisition: can compel town labor, at cost to Town.

## 9. Social conflicts
Aldmere vs Sorrel (grain vs coal, law vs speed, arms). Company vs Town (rents, flooding, wages). Town vs Hullfolk (moorings, theft accusations, ale). Concordance vs Company engineering (the spring). Hullfolk vs Company (clearance, the water debt).

## 10. Terminology
- **Watch**: a Keeper's shift; the game's turn.
- **Mark**: unit of water level on the sill gauge.
- **Pound**: a canal reach between locks; **the Pound** means the Summit Pound.
- **Paddles**: the sluice valves in the gates.
- **Holding**: a Hullfolk mooring right.
- **Scrip**: Company paper money, accepted at the Company store.
- **The Book**: the toll book. **The Ledger**: the Keeper's private record, passed on.
- **Irregular**: the Company's word for anything it does not want written down.
- **Going into the hill**: Hullfolk idiom for dying; literally, the river's fate.

## 12. Facts characters believe incorrectly
- Pell believes the reservoirs feed the pound and that the water fall is drought.
- Mirren believes her father left for Sorrel or was murdered by the Company.
- Hale believes the spring is natural and holy.
- Crane believes Aldmere could run the canal better with the same water.
- The town believes the Hullfolk are drifters from nowhere.
- Bram Holloway believes the Company *stole* the river deliberately to make the Hullfolk; it was Marrow alone, and the Company only inherited the lie.
