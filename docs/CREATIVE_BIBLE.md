# Creative Bible — HALFWAY LOCK

## 1. Title and subtitle
**HALFWAY LOCK** — *Keeper of the Meridian Canal*

## 2. One-sentence commercial hook
You are the keeper of the only lock that joins two rival shores, and every boat that asks to pass is a decision that someone will remember.

## 3. Store description
At the summit of the Meridian Canal sits Lock No. 9, called Halfway, the highest gate on the only water road across the continent. You are its new Keeper. Grain barges, smugglers, soldiers, pilgrims, auditors and the boat-clans who live on the water all arrive at your gates, and each one wants something that someone else will pay for. Swipe left or right to decide who passes, who waits, who pays and who is believed. Keep the summit water high enough to float a boat and low enough not to drown the town below, keep your employer satisfied and the townsfolk fed, and stay in post long enough to notice what every Keeper before you noticed too late: the water is falling, and nobody will say where it comes from. One thousand hand-written situations, twelve recurring characters, forty-two storylines and a mystery that unfolds across many Keepers make every run a different chapter of the same slow, strange history.

## 4. Player role and fantasy
The player is **the Keeper of Summit Lock No. 9**, appointed by the Meridian Canal Company. The fantasy is *the person at the chokepoint*: not a ruler, not a hero, but the one official whose small daily decisions have continental consequences. You are in the middle of everything and in charge of very little except the gates, the paddles, the toll book and your own reputation. Power comes from position, not rank, and every faction knows it.

Each run is a different Keeper. When a Keeper dies, is dismissed or leaves, the Company appoints another, and the town remembers the last one. Persistent discoveries are recorded in the Keeper's Ledger, handed from Keeper to Keeper.

## 5. Setting
The continent of **the Vell** is split by the **Spine**, a mountain range. East of the Spine lies **Aldmere**, old, agricultural and ruled by a Parliament of Guilds. West lies **Sorrel**, young, industrial and restless. The **Meridian Canal**, built eighty years ago by the chartered **Meridian Canal Company**, climbs the Spine in a staircase of locks from each side to the **Summit Pound**, a long artificial lake in a high valley. **Lock No. 9, Halfway**, is the top gate on the Aldmere side, and the town of **Halfway** has grown around it: an inn, a brewery, a chapel, a telegraph office, a boatyard, warehouses and the Company house.

Technology is steam, telegraph, oil lamps, iron and brass, printed newspapers, hand-cranked paddle gear and horse or steam haulage. No electricity in homes, no motor cars, no firearms more advanced than rifles and pepperbox pistols. The tone is invented late-industrial, not any real country or century.

## 6. Central conflict
Everyone needs the canal and nobody agrees what it is for. The Company needs revenue and order. Aldmere needs grain to move west and wants the canal under its own law. Sorrel needs coal and iron to move east and wants it faster and cheaper. The town of Halfway lives on the traffic but is flooded by it. The Hullfolk boat-clans live *on* the water and are the only people who remember what was here before it. All of these press on one Keeper who can open the gate, close it, or ask a question first.

## 7. Central mystery
**Where does the summit water come from, and why is it falling?**

The Company says the Summit Pound is fed by the Ninefold Reservoirs and the Cald Tarn aqueduct. The chapel says the springs are a gift. The truth, planned backward from the ending, is that the reservoirs have never held enough water. The founding engineer **Ysolde Marrow** secretly diverted the **River Quell** through a hidden tunnel, **the Marrow Cut**, drying the valley of **Lowmere** below the Spine. Lowmere's people became the landless Hullfolk. The Cut is now silting and failing, which is why the water falls a little every watch. The previous Keeper, **Ansel Dray**, worked this out and went into the Cut. The Company knows, and sends an auditor to watch every Keeper.

Clues are planted across the whole library: the daily fall of Water, the empty-sounding reservoir reports, the Hullfolk songs about a river, the brewery's "spring" that tastes of iron, Dray's coded ledger, the engineer's suppressed survey, the chapel's founding donation, the auditor's interest in the sluice house. The reveal reinterprets the Hullfolk from "vagrant boat people" into the dispossessed owners of the water.

## 8. Five primary resources
| Resource | Icon idea | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Water** | wave line with level mark | Level of the Summit Pound relative to the lock sills. Falls slowly every watch. |
| **Traffic** | two opposed arrows | Health of the flow of vessels. Low is a jammed queue; high is reckless throughput. |
| **Coffers** | a toll chest | The lock's own account: tolls, wages, repairs. |
| **Company** | a sealed charter | The Company's confidence in you as its Keeper. |
| **Town** | a lamp on a post | Halfway's regard for you: goodwill, labor, quiet nights. |

Hidden state also tracks each character's relationship, faction standings (Company, Town, Hullfolk, Aldmere, Sorrel, Concordance), a Suspicion counter (what the Company thinks you know), Fatigue, Evidence collected about the Cut, and dozens of flags.

## 9. Meaning of low and high values
All resources run 0–100. Ordinary runs begin at 50. Reaching 0 or 100 ends the run.

| Resource | 0 (failure) | Low band | High band | 100 (failure) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water | **The Dry Summit**: boats ground in the chamber, the canal closes, you are blamed. | Shallow drafts refuse, groundings, Hullfolk anger. | Wash over the towpath, cellars flood, chapel undercroft wet. | **The Breach**: the east embankment fails and Halfway's low streets are swept. |
| Traffic | **The Jam**: a month's boats moored nose to tail; Aldmere embargoes; you are removed. | Queues, tempers, bribes offered. | No inspections, night passages, contraband. | **The Pile-Up**: two vessels meet in the chamber; deaths; inquiry ends you. |
| Coffers | **Foreclosure**: wages unpaid, hands leave, the Company seizes the account and the post. | Repairs deferred, gates leak, hands grumble. | Rumors of skimming, thieves interested, Company curious. | **The Audit**: an unexplained surplus is proof of theft; arrested. |
| Company | **Dismissal**: your appointment is revoked by telegram. | Curt letters, an auditor's visit. | Praise, gifts, tasks that are not in the manual. | **The Promotion**: you are made the public face of the Company just as its reckoning arrives; the crowd finds you. |
| Town | **Run Out**: the town bars its doors, cuts the tow ropes and marches you to the boundary stone. | Cold shoulders, no hands for repairs, sabotage. | Speeches in your name, petitions, a bonfire. | **The Uprising**: the town rises against the Company in your name and you are hanged for it in the ledger of history, or exiled if the Teen rating is honored, which it is: charged with sedition and taken east in irons. |

Water drifts −1 every watch unless the mystery is resolved; this is the run's clock and the mystery in mechanical form.

## 10. Factions
| Faction | Wants | Fears | Distinct voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meridian Canal Company** | Revenue, order, the charter intact, the secret kept | Nationalization, the truth about the summit | Ledgers, forms, the word "irregular" |
| **Town of Halfway** | Work, dry cellars, cheap beer, respect | Being flooded, being bypassed | Practical, gossipy, resentful of uniforms |
| **The Hullfolk** | Passage without fees, their dead remembered, the water | Being cleared off the canal | Slow, proverb-rich, plural ("we hold") |
| **Aldmere** (east shore) | Grain moving, law, eventual ownership of the canal | Sorrel arms, Company impunity | Formal, statutory, dry |
| **Sorrel** (west shore) | Speed, coal, iron, cheaper tolls, a free press | Aldmere control, Company delays | Quick, commercial, impatient |
| **Concordance of the Still Water** | The springs honored, the chapel funded, mercy at the gate | The springs being merely engineering | Liturgical, gentle, evasive |

No faction is villainous. The Company built a wonder and lied to keep it. The Hullfolk are wronged and also smuggle. Aldmere's law is honest and would strangle Sorrel. Sorrel's freedom includes gun-running. The town is warm and sells stolen water. The chapel comforts people with a lie it half-believes.

## 11. Recurring gameplay loop
1. A card arrives: a vessel at the gate, a visitor at the Keeper's house, a telegram, a weather change, a discovery.
2. The player reads the speaker, the situation and, by dragging or focusing, the two decision labels and a preview of which resources will move and roughly how much.
3. The player commits by swipe, button or key.
4. Effects apply: resources, hidden relationships, flags, counters, delayed events, forced follow-ups.
5. The watch counter advances; Water drifts.
6. If a resource hits an edge or an ending condition triggers, the run ends with an ending card and a Ledger entry.

## 12. Run structure
- **Watch 1–8**: onboarding cards introduce the gates, the toll book, four of the characters and the falling Water.
- **Watch 9 onward**: evergreen, reactive and relationship cards, with minor arcs opening on conditions.
- **Every 20 watches**: a **Ledger Day** crisis check (audit, inspection, festival or storm), tuned by state.
- Major arcs open on relationship, flag or run-number conditions and can be interrupted and resumed.
- Runs typically last 40–120 watches. Death is common and informative. Each run's end writes to the Ledger.

## 13. Persistent metaprogression
- **The Keeper's Ledger**: discovered lore entries (facts about the Cut, the founding, the characters), unlocked across runs.
- **Run number**: later Keepers get different opening framings and cards that reference previous Keepers' fates.
- **Permanent unlocks**: e.g. *Dray's Key* (opens sluice-house cards), *Quenn's Survey*, *The Lowmere Song*, *Marrow's Letters*. Each unlock enables new cards and lets a true-ending route start.
- **Character gallery** and **ending history**.
- **Objectives**: named goals ("Find the second half of the ledger cipher") that guide play without forcing it.

## 14. Narrative tone
Dry, humane, observant. Melancholy under bustle. The canal is beautiful and the work is absurd. People are funny because they are specific, not because they quip.

## 15. Humor principles
- Comedy from **situation** (a barge of geese, a duel over mooring order), **personality** (Pell's boosterism, Matthias's love of statutes), **contrast** (a holy blessing of a coal barge) and **consequence** (the geese come back).
- Characters are funny in different ways: Pell is expansive, Matthias is deadpan, Nessa is quick, Fennick is indiscreet, Quenn is anxious. Bram, Ilse, Mirren and Odile are rarely funny on purpose.
- No jokes during deaths, refugee cards, the drowning of the valley, or the true-ending cards.

## 16. Emotional pillars
- **Responsibility without authority**.
- **The comfort of routine and the cost of it**.
- **Belated recognition**: understanding what was in front of you all along.
- **Small mercy**: the hidden power to let someone through.

## 17. Themes
Infrastructure as buried history; who owns water; the price of a shortcut; institutional memory versus personal memory; the dignity of the gatekeeper.

## 18. Content boundaries (Teen / 13+)
Death is described, not depicted; no gore. Violence is off-card or brief. No sexual content; romance is implied and adult. Alcohol exists (a brewery town) but is not glamorized. No slurs. No real-world religions, nations or people. Refugee and dispossession themes are handled seriously. No self-harm content.

## 19. Target audience
Players of short-session narrative games on phone and desktop, 13+, who like systems with stories in them; fans of literary strategy, canal and railway history, quiet mysteries.

## 20. Distinctive selling points
1. A ticking primary resource that *is* the mystery.
2. Two-directional danger on every resource; no "max everything" play.
3. Forty-two interlocking storylines with delayed and cross-run consequences.
4. Characters whose voices are identifiable with names hidden.
5. Fully offline, data-driven, moddable content with a documented schema and validators.

## 21. How this differs from other binary-decision games
No monarch, no court, no four humors of state. The player is a mid-level employee of a company, not a sovereign, and the antagonism is structural rather than personal. Resources fail at both ends and interact (opening the gate spends Water for Traffic). The run's clock is a mystery rather than a lifespan. The metagame is investigative: unlocks are *facts*, and facts change which endings are possible. Each run is a *different person* in the same chair, so the town and the characters remember previous Keepers.

## 22. Visual pitch
Lamplit brass and wet stone at altitude: a portrait-format card with a character framed like a photograph in a Company file, set against a long horizontal water line that literally rises and falls with the Water resource. The palette is deep pound-water green, wet slate, oil-lamp amber and the pale chalk of Company paper. Characters are drawn in flat inked shapes with two-tone shading and one accent color per faction, so a silhouette tells you who is speaking before you read.

## 23. Audio pitch
A small ensemble of bowed and plucked strings, a harmonium that breathes like a sluice, and recorded-style water and iron textures. Ordinary play is quiet: dripping, gear clicks, distant tow horses, a low drone that thins as the Water falls. Crisis music is a fast harmonium pulse with a single struck bell; endings resolve to the Hullfolk river theme played on a single fiddle.
